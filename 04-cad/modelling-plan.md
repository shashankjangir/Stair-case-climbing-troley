# Trolley Modelling Plan — Blender implementation and SolidWorks reference

Base dimensions from the [research and CAD design basis](../03-research/trolley-research-and-cad-design-basis.md). Units: mm, kg, N. In SolidWorks use MMGS; the active Blender implementation stores geometry in metres and displays millimetres.

## Active workflow — 10 October 2026

The current deliverable is an editable Blender assembly in `blender/stair-trolley.blend`, generated by `scripts/build_blender.py` using `scripts/params.py`. Previews are in `previews/` and check reports in `checks/`. The SolidWorks filenames and operations below remain a reference for a later native CAD implementation; they are not files already produced. Blender objects and collections represent the parts and subassemblies. This first pass does not provide SolidWorks mates, STEP solids, validated contact simulation or structural analysis.

The first model includes the frame, one shared axle, two independently controllable clusters, six wheels, four plates, four bearing envelopes, toe platform, handle, hidden payload reference and hidden nominal stair environment. Threads, bearing fits, split-clamp fasteners, exact root blends, restraint hardware and weld details remain pending. Core source parameters and explicit layout values in the generator define this first pass; not every dimension is yet linked parametrically. Rotating cluster/wheel pivot objects is available directly in Blender.

Modelled extra choices: 25 mm handle bar with 32 mm grip sleeves, 6 mm hub flanges, Ø70.5 plate centre opening and Ø20.5 wheel-axle clearance holes. These are preliminary modelling choices, not finished machining tolerances.

## Coordinate system

Origin = main axle centre.

| Axis | Direction |
|------|-----------|
| +x   | Toward the payload / toe |
| −x   | Toward the operator / stair ascent |
| +y   | Trolley right side (looking from operator) |
| −y   | Trolley left side |
| +z   | Upward when frame is upright |

**Use global variables / equations** so clearance corrections don't require rebuilding.

## Part-by-part build sequence

### Phase 1 — Core mechanism parts

#### Part 1: Wheel (`wheel.sldprt`)
- Revolve a Ø200 × 50 mm tread profile around the wheel axis
- Ø20 bore through-hole
- 60 mm hub length
- Material: rubber tread appearance and separate wheel-centre envelope. Wheel mass remains unverified; do not assign a supplier mass from appearance or use 1.2 kg as a confirmed value.
- This is a purchased part — model the **dimensional envelope** only

#### Part 2: Carrier plate (`carrier-plate.sldprt`)
- 6 mm thick IS 2062 E250 steel
- Three swept arms from central hub region to wheel centres
  - Wheel centres at polar angles 0°, 120°, 240° from centre, at radius R = 190 mm
  - Each arm starts at radius 55 mm, offset 15° behind its wheel
  - 50 mm wide arm envelope, smooth centreline to wheel centre
  - Ø60 end bosses at each wheel position
  - ≥25 mm root blend radius where arms meet the hub ring
- Central ring: Ø110 outer, Ø70.5 clearance opening for the Ø70 sleeve, with 6 × Ø9 bolt holes on 90 PCD. Add three Ø20.5 wheel-axle holes. These clearance sizes are starting choices, not final bearing fits.
- Use the same in-plane profile for both plates in a cluster so the wheel-axle holes align. Mirroring across the trolley mid-plane changes the assembly side, not the wheel-hole locations. Do not mirror the curved profile in its own plane without checking the paired layout.
- **Four plates total** (two per cluster)

#### Part 3: Central hub (`central-hub.sldprt`)
- Turned steel sleeve
- OD = 70 mm, length = 76 mm
- Two bearing seats: Ø52 × 15 mm deep at each end
- Central relief/shoulder geometry between seats
- Inner-race spacer length = 46 mm nominal (between inner races)
- Two Ø110 × 6 flanges per hub, each with 6 × M8 tapped holes on 90 PCD. Relative to the cluster centre, flanges occupy y = −32 to −26 and +26 to +32; mating plates occupy −38 to −32 and +32 to +38. This retains the 64 mm inner-face plate gap and 76 mm outer-face stack. Central sleeve passes through the plate openings.
- Central sleeve through-bore: Ø40 between bearing shoulders; bearing seats occupy y = −38 to −23 and +23 to +38. Inner-race spacer: nominal Ø36 outer, Ø25.2 inner, 46 long. Verify fits and retention before drawings.
- Bolt heads, shoulders and nuts are envelopes in Blender; threads and end-retention details are not yet modelled.
- Material: mild steel (E250)
- Note: turn seats after any welding

#### Part 4: Main axle (`main-axle.sldprt`)
- Solid Ø25 mm, overall length 660 mm
- Machined seats for bearing inner races
- Positive end-retention features (circlips / threaded ends)
- Material: C45 (≥300 MPa yield)
- **Do not weld this part to the frame**

#### Part 5: Wheel axle (`wheel-axle.sldprt`)
- Ø20 with shoulders
- ~70 mm support-centre span
- Positive locking feature
- Final length depends on bearing/washer stack — leave parametric
- Material: C45
- Quantity: 6

#### Part 6: Cluster bearing (`bearing-6205.sldprt`)
- 6205 deep-groove: 25 × 52 × 15 mm
- Model as an envelope (rectangle revolve with bore and OD)
- Sealed variant; confirm suffix with supplier
- Quantity: 4 (two per cluster)

### Phase 2 — Sub-assembly: Tri-star cluster

#### Assembly: `tri-star-cluster.sldasm`
1. Fix the hub at origin
2. Mate two carrier plates to hub flanges (bolted, plate_gap = 64 mm between inner faces)
3. Insert three wheel axles through paired plate end-bosses at R = 190 from centre
4. Mount one wheel on each axle (concentric + width mates)
5. Insert two 6205 bearings into hub seats
6. Keep the main axle out of the cluster subassembly. Reference its axis here; add the one shared axle only in the complete trolley assembly.

**Check:** cluster must rotate freely around the main axle. Each wheel must spin independently on its axle.

**Swept diameter** = 2 × (R + r) = 2 × (190 + 100) = **580 mm** — verify no part exceeds this.

---

### Phase 3 — Frame

#### Part 7: Frame weldment (`chassis-frame.sldprt` — use SolidWorks Weldment feature)

Structural members:
- Two uprights at x = 100 mm, y = ±(420/2 − 35/2) = ±192.5 (centreline to centreline)
- Cross-members connecting uprights at top and bottom
- Main-frame tube: 35 × 35 × 2 mm square tube
- Total upright height: from toe platform (~z = −155) to handle bar region (~z = 1005)

Axle support brackets:
- At y = ±195, mounted to frame
- Split clamps to hold Ø25 main axle
- Screen each bracket for the specified 1412.64 N one-sided vertical load and its actual offset. The 78.28 MPa figure is the **main axle bending stress**, not a bracket strength result. Split clamps and bracket welds need their own checks.

Material: IS 4923 YSt 240 (yield ≥240 MPa, tensile ≥410 MPa)

#### Part 8: Toe platform
- Two toe rails: 30 × 30 × 2 mm tube, 300 mm long, x = 100 to 400
- Deck plate: 400 × 300 × 2 mm E250 steel on top of rails, top at z = −155
- Two gussets: 100 × 100 × 4 mm triangular, E250, welded at toe-to-upright joints
- Deck top is z = −155, deck underside −157, rail centre −172 and rail underside **−187**. With the axle 195 mm above the floor, nominal clearance is **8 mm**. Raising the deck or changing the rails would be necessary to obtain 10 mm floor clearance; do not silently change the specified deck height.
- Include a preliminary central 30 × 30 × 2 deck-support rail. Check plate bending and all toe welds under the intended load; its presence is not a load-rating verification.

#### Part 9: Handle
- Steel crossbar at z = 1005, x = 40
- Grip width = 380 mm (centred on trolley)
- Round grip sleeve over steel bar (model the sleeve OD)
- Smooth closed ends — no exposed tube edges
- Approximately 1200 mm above nominal floor contact

### Phase 4 — Complete assembly

#### Assembly: `stair-trolley-assembly.sldasm`

1. Fix the chassis frame at origin
2. Insert main axle into frame brackets (fixed — the axle does not rotate)
3. Insert left tri-star cluster at y = −280 (concentric with axle, free to rotate)
4. Insert right tri-star cluster at y = +280 (concentric with axle, free to rotate, **independent** of left)
5. Add toe platform to frame
6. Add handle to frame
7. Add a reference payload envelope 280 mm deep × 300 mm wide × 300 mm high, spanning x = 120 to 400 so it clears the upright front face at x = 117.5. The Blender object records a nominal 50 kg payload property; measured mass/CG calculations and 10/25/40/50 kg configurations are still pending.
8. Add two strap paths and four attachment points (model as reference geometry)

## Assembly checks (from research §8)

### Priority: wheel-first stair contact

Shashank reported that another first-year group's similar trolley caught its frame on the stairs before its leading tyre could engage. The [observation and annotated preview](../01-problems/README.md#frame-hitting-the-stairs-before-the-wheels--observation-from-another-group) identify the axle-to-frame connection as the area to reconsider.

Treat the current 100 mm axle-to-upright X offset as **unvalidated**, not a final dimension. This longitudinal offset is different from the 560 mm lateral wheel track and 660 mm axle length; increasing those lateral dimensions does not resolve a side-view frame/step collision.

Before finalising this connection:

1. Show the stair approach direction explicitly in a side view. Check the intended backward-pulled ascent, first-step engagement, repeated transitions and landing exit; evaluate descent separately.
2. Place the trolley at its operating tilt and vary the carrier angle. Identify first contact with each riser and nosing, including the lower frame, toe, gussets and axle brackets.
3. Adjust the longitudinal axle/frame offset and, if needed, the toe position so a tyre reaches the step before any rigid body part. Judge the actual step geometry, not just which part protrudes farthest in the preview.
4. Maintain the selected 10 mm nominal rigid-part/stair clearance through the contact sequence. The separate 8 mm flat-floor clearance is not evidence that this stair requirement passes.
5. Recalculate bracket bending, frame loads, CG and handle effort after changing the offset. A longer connection changes the load path as well as the clearance.

No replacement offset is selected yet. Record the interference results and chosen dimension before changing the main assembly; do not present a guessed extension as a verified solution.

Run these **before** producing fabrication drawings:

- [ ] **Mass and CG** — empty, 10, 25, 40, 50 kg; include lateral offsets and tall/forward loads
- [ ] **Motion study** — complete contact sequence: first-step engagement → repeated steps → top landing
- [ ] **All five stair cases** — Cases A–E, with nosing variants
- [ ] **Unequal cluster phasing** — left and right clusters rotate independently; check crossed positions
- [ ] **Clearance** — ≥10 mm between carrier/hub/axle brackets and stairs throughout motion
- [ ] **Handle lever arm plot** — lever arm, load moment, contact forces vs. carrier angle
- [ ] **Interference detection** — SolidWorks Interference Detection on full assembly at multiple positions
- [ ] **Negative contact reactions** — flag positions requiring unrealistic friction or lift-off
- [ ] **Frame torsion** — one-sided loading, factored loads
- [ ] **Grip access** — can the operator reach and hold comfortably?
- [ ] **Pinch points** — no finger-trap hazards between moving parts
- [ ] **Payload retention** — straps secure the load at all tilt angles
- [ ] **Landing turning** — can the trolley turn on a 1200 × 1200 mm landing?
- [ ] **Descent** — space near both hubs for future control mechanism; trolley is **not self-braking**
