# Project Background

I first worked on the Staircase Climbing Trolley in my first year, in October 2024, with Prof. Sagar Sarkar. The project involved a tri-star wheel mechanism, chassis CAD, mechanical analysis, fabrication, and prototype testing.

At that time, I was not familiar with GitHub and did not keep the work documented there. I am now working through the project again from the beginning, with a clearer record of the design and the results.

## What I want to achieve now

I want to develop a working payload trolley with a three-wheel tri-star cluster mechanism and understand the engineering behind it. That includes the geometry needed to cross the selected stairs, the torque and pull force required, and the effect of load placement on balance.

The work will include a complete CAD assembly, structural and stability checks, fabrication, and tests with secured payloads during movement. The finished prototype should be assessed for smooth stair transitions, structural rigidity, rollover tendency, and manual pulling effort compared with a regular trolley.

These are the objectives of the current work. The results will be recorded as the calculations, build, and tests are completed.

## Earlier material

The original [MCP101 project report](original-prototype/report/mcp101-project-report.docx), titled *Staircase Climbing Trolley with Tri-Wheel Mechanism*, is now available along with its four prototype photographs. The cover identifies Groups 37 and 38 and six team members. The work is recorded here as a group project; individual task ownership is not specified in the report.

The report describes frame and wheel-assembly modelling, stress and strain simulation, frame cutting and welding, mechanism assembly, stair trials, and adjustments to wheel spacing and handle design. The photographs document a physical rectangular frame with triangular three-wheel carriers on both sides.

It reports smooth stair transitions and reduced user effort, and mentions a 30–50 kg load-testing range with a platform intended for up to 50 kg. It also records slight tilting with heavier loads, traction limitations on wet or slippery surfaces, and material cost concerns.

The report does not include dimensions, final material specifications, CAD files, numerical simulation results, torque calculations, force measurements, or detailed test logs. These are the next records to recover or produce during the rebuild. The earlier observations inform the work without replacing measurements from the current build.

Motor assistance, folding, improved traction, braking, and load restraints appear as future directions in the report. They are not recorded as completed features.

## How the project will develop

I will update the existing notes as I learn and make progress. If I later arrive at a substantially different idea, I will keep the existing design and develop the new one separately so the two can be understood and compared.
