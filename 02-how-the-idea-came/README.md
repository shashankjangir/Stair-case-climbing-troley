# How the Idea Came

I first worked on the Staircase Climbing Trolley in my first year, in October 2024, with Prof. Sagar Sarkar. I was not familiar with GitHub at the time, so I did not keep the work documented there. I am now taking the project from the beginning again and recording the decisions along the way.

## The idea I am working on

The starting concept is a three-wheel tri-star cluster. I want to explore how the wheels and rotating carrier can help the trolley move from one step to the next, instead of relying on the operator to lift a regular trolley over every edge.

The mechanism is only one part of the design. The chassis, handle, load position, and wheel arrangement need to work together so the trolley remains manageable throughout a flight of stairs.

## What I want to work out

- How should the wheel size and cluster spacing relate to the stairs?
- Where should the load sit so the trolley remains balanced during climbing?
- How should the handle position support a manageable pulling posture?
- What torque and pull force are needed at the difficult points of the motion?
- Can the frame carry the chosen payload without excessive deflection?
- Will a built prototype show a measurable improvement over a regular trolley?

## The concept we built earlier

In the first-year group project, we built a rectangular frame with a three-wheel cluster on each side. The report describes frame cutting and welding, assembly of the wheel mechanism, and adjustments to wheel spacing and handle design. The photographs show the frame and the triangular wheel carriers positioned on stairs.

That experience is the starting point for the current work. The next design decisions should address the balance and traction issues recorded in the report while keeping fabrication practical.

The original report suggested motor assistance, a foldable frame, improved traction, braking, and load restraints as future improvements. These are historical suggestions, not built features. The current rebuild is explicitly non-electric. A longer braced axle-to-frame connection and a mechanical assisted-tilt idea were discussed on 10 October 2026; no modification or performance claim has been made for them.
